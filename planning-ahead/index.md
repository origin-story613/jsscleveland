Planning ahead is a kindness to the people you love. When your wishes are written down and your family knows them, they can focus on mourning rather than on decisions made under pressure.

## Pre-planning checklist

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>Complete a Halachic Living Will</strong> and name a health-care proxy who will honor Jewish law. <a href="{{ '/resources/halachic-living-wills/' | relative_url }}">Download the Ohio directive</a></li>
  <li><strong>Carry an EMES card</strong> so first responders and family know your wishes. <a href="{{ '/resources/emes-card/' | relative_url }}">About the EMES card</a></li>
  <li><strong>Put your burial wishes in writing</strong>, including your wish for tahara and a traditional Jewish burial. <a href="{{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}">Request our burial wishes form</a></li>
  <li><strong>Choose a cemetery and purchase a plot.</strong> <a href="{{ '/burial-arrangements/area-cemeteries/' | relative_url }}">Area cemeteries</a> · <a href="{{ '/burial-arrangements/burial-in-israel/' | relative_url }}">Burial in Israel</a></li>
  <li><strong>Write down your Hebrew name</strong> and your parents' Hebrew names.</li>
  <li><strong>Tell your family and your rabbi</strong> where your documents are kept and what you wish.</li>
  <li><strong>Consider pre-arranging with a funeral home</strong> so your wishes are on file. <a href="{{ '/when-a-death-occurs/funeral-homes/' | relative_url }}">Working with funeral homes</a></li>
</ul>

## Starting the conversation

Talking about death can feel uncomfortable, but it is one of the most caring conversations a family can have. It can help to begin with a practical step, like filling out the Halachic Living Will together, or to ask your rabbi to join the conversation.

If you or a family member is considering cremation, please read [Burial vs Cremation]({{ '/education/cremation/' | relative_url }}) and speak with us or your rabbi.
