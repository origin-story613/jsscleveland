Spellings vary; Ashkenazi pronunciation is shown in parentheses where it is commonly used.

<dl class="glossary">
  <dt>Aninut <span>(aninus)</span></dt>
  <dd>The period between death and burial. A close relative in this period is an <em>onen</em>.</dd>

  <dt>Aron</dt>
  <dd>The casket: a plain wooden box with no metal. <a href="{{ '/education/what-is-jewish-burial/' | relative_url }}#aron-the-casket">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Avel <span>(plural: aveilim)</span></dt>
  <dd>A mourner, after the burial. Mourning is observed for a parent, spouse, child or sibling.</dd>

  <dt>Chevra Kadisha</dt>
  <dd>"Holy society": the volunteers who prepare the deceased for burial according to Jewish law.</dd>

  <dt>Hesped</dt>
  <dd>A eulogy delivered at the funeral.</dd>

  <dt>Kaddish</dt>
  <dd>A prayer praising G-d, recited by mourners in the presence of a minyan.</dd>

  <dt>Kevod HaMet <span>(kavod hames)</span></dt>
  <dd>"Honor of the deceased": the principle that the body is treated with dignity and respect. <a href="{{ '/education/holiness-of-body/' | relative_url }}">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Keriah</dt>
  <dd>The tearing of a garment by mourners, as a sign of grief, before the funeral.</dd>

  <dt>Kvura <span>(kevurah)</span></dt>
  <dd>Burial. <em>Kvura bikarka</em> means burial in the ground. <a href="{{ '/education/what-is-jewish-burial/' | relative_url }}#kvura-bikarka-in-ground-burial">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Levaya</dt>
  <dd>The funeral; literally "accompanying" the deceased to their resting place.</dd>

  <dt>Matzeiva <span>(matzeivah)</span></dt>
  <dd>The headstone placed on the grave.</dd>

  <dt>Met <span>(meis)</span></dt>
  <dd>The deceased.</dd>

  <dt>Minyan</dt>
  <dd>A quorum of ten adult Jewish men, required for Kaddish and communal prayer.</dd>

  <dt>Seudat Havra'ah</dt>
  <dd>The meal of condolence, prepared by others for the mourners on returning from the burial.</dd>

  <dt>Shiva</dt>
  <dd>The seven days of mourning after the burial. <a href="{{ '/mourning/' | relative_url }}">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Shloshim</dt>
  <dd>The thirty days of mourning, counted from the burial.</dd>

  <dt>Shmira <span>(shemira)</span></dt>
  <dd>Watching over the deceased from death until burial. The watcher is a <em>shomer</em>. <a href="{{ '/education/what-is-jewish-burial/' | relative_url }}#shmira-the-vigil">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Tachrichim</dt>
  <dd>Simple white linen burial shrouds. <a href="{{ '/education/what-is-jewish-burial/' | relative_url }}#tachrichim-the-shroud">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Tahara</dt>
  <dd>The ritual washing and purification of the deceased by the Chevra Kadisha. <a href="{{ '/education/what-is-jewish-burial/' | relative_url }}#tahara-the-preparation">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Tehillim</dt>
  <dd>The Book of Psalms, customarily recited beside the deceased and by those praying for the sick.</dd>

  <dt>Unveiling</dt>
  <dd>A short ceremony dedicating the headstone.</dd>

  <dt>Yahrtzeit</dt>
  <dd>The anniversary of a death on the Hebrew calendar. <a href="{{ '/resources/yahrtzeit/' | relative_url }}">More</a></dd>

  <dt>Yizkor</dt>
  <dd>The memorial prayer recited in synagogue on Yom Kippur, Shemini Atzeret, the last day of Pesach and the second day of Shavuot.</dd>
</dl>
